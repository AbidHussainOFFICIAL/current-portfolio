Every project I take on starts the same way: a pen, a sheet of paper and no screen in sight. It looks old-fashioned, but it is the cheapest way I know to find out whether an idea works.

## Paper is fast and forgiving

A rough layout takes a minute to draw and a second to throw away. In Figma or in code, even a quick layout starts to feel precious, because you have already spent time on it. On paper there is nothing to protect.

## What I settle before opening a laptop

- What the page is for, and the one thing a visitor should do next
- The order of the content, from the first screen to the last
- Where the layout needs to change on a phone
- Which parts need motion, and which are better left still

> If the idea survives ten minutes of sketching, it usually survives the build.

## Keeping the sketches

I photograph the best sketches and keep them next to the design file. When a question comes up months later, the paper version often explains why a decision was made.

None of this replaces proper design or testing. It simply means the expensive steps start with fewer wrong turns.
