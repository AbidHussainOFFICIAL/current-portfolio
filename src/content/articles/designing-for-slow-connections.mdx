Plenty of visitors are on a weak mobile signal, a crowded café network or an older phone. Designing for them makes the site better for everyone.

## Start with a budget

I set limits before building: a small amount of JavaScript on first load, one font family loaded in two files and images that never exceed the size they are displayed at.

## Habits that help

- Serve images in modern formats and at the right size
- Load fonts with a sensible fallback so text appears immediately
- Keep animation on the compositor, using transform and opacity only
- Delay anything below the fold until the visitor scrolls near it
- Make every important action work before the extra scripts arrive

## Test the real thing

Throttling the network in the browser tools is a good start, but nothing beats trying the site on an actual mid-range phone with a patchy connection.

> A fast site is a design decision, not a last-minute cleanup.

If a feature cannot be made fast, I either simplify it or offer a lighter version. Visitors rarely notice the effects that were left out, but they always notice a slow page.
